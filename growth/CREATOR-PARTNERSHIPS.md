# Creator partnerships — the pitch and the short-list

The single biggest lever in the corpus, and the one DraftToDone is best placed to pull.

Loïc's Influify plateaued at €30K/month. He merged with one large e-commerce YouTuber, became
Minéa, and went **0 → €100K MRR on that creator's audience alone** — off one live stream. He
then repeated it deliberately with DropMagic: a YouTuber in the founding team from day one,
€1K MRR in five days, €83K/month in seven months.

The KDP niche is unusually well supplied with exactly this kind of creator. They already rank
for the buyer-intent queries, they already monetise via affiliate links, and their audience is
precisely the ICP.

---

## Why a creator is worth more than a commission

Three things, and most people only count the first:

1. **Distribution.** Obvious. A baseline of revenue before you spend anything.
2. **They are your best power user.** They live in KDP daily; you have one banned account and
   a spreadsheet. Product feedback from an insider, continuously, is worth more than the reach.
3. **They hand you the unit economics.** After one video with an insider you know your cost
   per view, your conversion rate and your LTV — so every subsequent creator deal is bought on
   data instead of hope.

---

## The ladder — do not start at the top

Loïc's exact sequence, and the order matters because each rung pays for the next.

| Rung | Who | Deal | Purpose |
|------|-----|------|---------|
| 1 | **Under 10K subs** | Pure commission, 40% lifetime, nothing upfront | Zero downside. Validates that the offer converts at all. |
| 2 | **10K–50K subs** | Cash upfront + commission | They have real production costs, so the risk moves to them. Only reach here once rung 1 has converted. |
| 3 | **The one that works** | Lock for a quarter, several videos | One video is noise. A sequence is a channel. |
| 4 | **Ambassador** | Revenue share or equity | The tool is embedded in their workflow, not sponsored. This is the Minéa outcome. |

**Do not skip to rung 2.** Paying upfront before you know the offer converts is how founders
lose their runway to fake influencers — Roman's stated mistake.

## Where to find them

The named channels below came out of a search on 2026-07-26. **Verify each one before you
send anything** — subscriber counts move, channels go dormant, and the fit matters more than
the size. Treat this as a starting point for the method, not a finished list.

### English — the operator/low-content side (best fit)

- **Self Publishing Profits** (Emeka Ossai) — nonfiction and low-content KDP, income-focused.
  Closest audience match to our ICP.
- **The Home Boss** (Nuria) — low-content KDP, high output, very tool-friendly.
- **Ashley Schmitt** — low-content books on KDP as an income stream.
- **Ken | Self-Publishing Secrets** — full-time income from KDP. Smaller channel, which by the
  ladder makes it a *better* rung-1 target, not a worse one.
- **Ben McQueeney** — low-content and puzzle publishing; already recommends KDP tools, so the
  affiliate motion is established.

### English — the craft/author side (secondary)

- **Kindlepreneur** (Dave Chesson) — the authority in book marketing and KDP keywords. Big,
  hard to reach, and he sells Publisher Rocket, so treat as a long-term relationship rather
  than a rung-1 target.
- **Self-Publishing Made Simple** (April Cox) — aimed at new authors. Weaker ICP fit; a
  recommendation here reaches people who want to write one book.

### Adjacent, non-YouTube

- **Self Publishing Titans** — tools plus a masterclass, and an existing audience of
  operators. A partnership or a listing is probably worth more than a video.
- **Low Content Profits** — reviews KDP products. Exactly the sort of site that should be
  running a DraftToDone review.

### French (your home advantage)

The French KDP scene is much thinner, which cuts both ways: fewer targets, but far less
competition for their attention, and you can do the whole relationship in your first language.
**Créaction** (`@Cre.action`) covers KDP and passive income in French and is a reasonable first
call. Beyond that, build the list yourself — search French for KDP, auto-édition and low
content, and sort by recent upload date rather than subscriber count.

### The repeatable sourcing method (better than any list)

1. Search YouTube for the buyer-intent queries: *"best AI book generator"*, *"KDP tools 2026"*,
   *"how to make a KDP cover"*, *"low content book tutorial"*. Whoever ranks is already
   reaching people at the moment of purchase.
2. Filter by **recent uploads**, not subscribers. A dormant 100K channel is worth less than an
   active 4K one.
3. Check whether they already run affiliate links in descriptions. If they do, the motion is
   established and you are a line item, not a new idea.
4. Read their comments. That is the ICP talking, and it tells you which angle to pitch.
5. Log: channel, size, last upload, existing affiliations, angle, date contacted, reply.

## The pitch

Short, specific, and it leads with what they get. Do not attach a media kit to a cold email.

> **Subject:** free access + 40% for your audience?
>
> Hi {name},
>
> I watched your video on {specific video — name it, and say one true thing about it}.
>
> I built DraftToDone: you give it a niche brief and it produces the whole KDP package —
> manuscript, full wrap cover, title, subtitle, description, the 7 keyword slots, and a pen
> name. I ran a KDP catalog to €400/month myself before Amazon banned my account over a bad
> pen name, so the tool is built around not repeating that.
>
> Two things, no strings on either:
>
> 1. A free account, permanently. Use it, break it, tell me it's rubbish if it is.
> 2. If you do end up recommending it, 40% recurring for as long as your referral stays —
>    about €156 a year per customer on the annual plan.
>
> No script, no approval process, and I'd genuinely rather you say the limitations on camera
> than not. Want the account?
>
> Antoine
> drafttodone.io/en/partners

### Why it is written that way

- **Name a specific video.** Anything generic reads as a mail merge, because it is one.
- **The free account is the ask, not the deal.** Saying yes to a free account costs nothing —
  this is Roman's blueprint principle applied to creators. The commission is mentioned and
  then dropped.
- **"Tell me it's rubbish if it is."** Creators are pitched constantly by people who cannot
  tolerate criticism. This inverts it and is the line most likely to get a reply.
- **The ban story does the credibility work.** It says you are an operator, not a marketer.
- **"No script, no approval process."** The most common reason a creator declines is the fear
  of being managed.

## The video that actually converts

Sleek's most important finding, and it is counterintuitive: **a creator showcasing your product
for ten minutes converts badly.** What converts is a **full-workflow video** — *"how I go from
a niche idea to a finished book on KDP"* — where your tool is simply what gets used at one step.

A single workflow video of that shape (Starter Story, ~120K views) brought Sleek an estimated
100–150 customers, roughly €4–5K MRR.

So when you brief a creator, ask for the workflow, not the review. It is better for them too:
workflow videos rank and keep earning, whereas tool reviews die with the tool.

## What to send once they say yes

The kit, not a link. Roman's point is that affiliates fail because nobody tells them how to
promote — so the whole kit is already described on
[`/partners`](https://drafttodone.io/en/partners) and needs to actually exist before the first
creator says yes:

- [ ] A 60–90 second demo video, clean screen recording, no voiceover, so they can drop it into their own edit
- [ ] The complete output of one real book — manuscript, cover, metadata — as downloadable files to show on screen
- [ ] Copy blocks for YouTube descriptions, X, and a newsletter slot
- [ ] The angles that convert for this audience, and the ones that do not
- [ ] Their affiliate link and a live dashboard

**Build this before the outreach, not after.** A creator who says yes and then waits a week for
assets has already lost interest.
