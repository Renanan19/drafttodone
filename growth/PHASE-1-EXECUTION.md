# Phase 1 — the first 10 paying operators

Everything here is meant to be copied and sent, not admired. Target: **10 paying customers
in 30 days**, using direct response only. See [`STRATEGY.md`](./STRATEGY.md) for why this
phase comes before anything else.

The ICP, everywhere below: **a KDP catalog operator** — someone publishing multiple
non-fiction titles on Amazon, who already knows what a keyword slot and a royalty rate are.
Not a novelist.

---

## Play 1 — The free generation offer

**This is the single highest-leverage thing available and it costs credits, not money.**

Sleek used exactly this to go from nothing to €10K/month: *"comment your app idea and we'll
design it for you free"* — run manually, handed back as a finished project. It removes 100% of
the prospect's friction, and each reply is a warm lead who has now seen the output quality.

For DraftToDone it is better than it was for Sleek, because the deliverable is a whole book
package rather than a mockup.

**Where to post it:** X, LinkedIn, r/selfpublish, r/KDP, and the KDP Facebook groups.

### The post

> I built an AI that produces a complete KDP package from one niche brief — manuscript, full
> wrap cover, title, subtitle, description, 7 keyword slots, pen name.
>
> I want to know where it breaks before I sell more of it.
>
> Comment your niche and I'll run it and send you the whole package. Free, no signup, nothing
> to unsubscribe from. Yours to publish if it's good, and I'd rather you tell me if it isn't.
>
> First 20.

### Why it is worded that way

- *"I want to know where it breaks"* — the ask is honest, so it does not read as a funnel.
- *"Yours to publish"* — the gift is real and unconditional. Anything less and nobody comments.
- *"First 20"* — capped, so it is credible and so you can actually deliver.
- No link. The link kills reach on every platform, and the comments are the point.

### What to do with every reply

1. Generate it. Send it back **inside 24 hours** — speed is most of the impression.
2. Ask one question, always the same one: **"What would have to be true for you to upload this?"**
   That answer is your roadmap, your objection list and your landing-page copy.
3. Log it: niche, what they said, whether they published it.
4. Do not pitch. If the package is good they will ask what it costs. If they do not ask, the
   product is not ready and you have just learned the most valuable thing available.

This play doubles as Rob's step 6 — *talk to 50 customers* — except these are free and warm.

## Play 2 — Reddit, three posts a week

Roman got 11M impressions in four months on this, free. The rule is **story + proof + link**,
and every business experiment becomes a post.

Subreddits: `r/selfpublish` · `r/KDP` · `r/Kindle` · `r/publishing` · `r/passive_income` ·
`r/Entrepreneur` · `r/SideProject`

Read each one's self-promo rules first. Most allow this if the post carries real value and the
link is incidental. **Do not** run upvote rings — it is vote manipulation, and it gets the
account and eventually the domain burned.

### Four posts you can already write, because they already happened

**1. The ban.**
> *"Amazon permanently banned my KDP account over a pen name. Here is exactly what I did wrong."*
Post the pen name, the email, the timeline. This is the strongest asset you have and almost
nobody in this niche will publish theirs. It is useful, it is specific, and it establishes that
you have operated a real catalog.

**2. The teardown.**
> *"I had AI write a complete 38,000-word book. Here are the 6 things it got wrong that would
> have got it rejected."*
Screenshot the actual failures. This post is credible precisely because it argues against your
own product's naive version — and it sets up why review-before-upload is the whole point.

**3. The economics.**
> *"I ran the numbers on a 40-book KDP catalog. Here is what each book actually has to earn."*
Real spreadsheet, real royalty math, real conclusion — including the books that lost money.

**4. The metadata experiment.**
> *"I changed only the subtitle on 5 books. Here is what happened to the rankings in 30 days."*
Run it first, then post it. This one takes a month of lead time, so start it today.

### Format, every time

Story in the first two lines. Proof in the middle — screenshots, numbers, the actual document.
Conclusion someone can use without buying anything. Link at the end, once, to the **playbook**
(not the pricing page).

## Play 3 — Cold outreach with the blueprint

Roman's finding, which he later told Florian was his single most profitable channel: **do not
ask for a demo, offer a blueprint.** Saying yes to a document is easy. Most people never book
the call — they go start a trial instead.

The blueprint already exists: `drafttodone.io/en/kdp-operator-playbook`.

### Where the list comes from

- Authors with 3+ non-fiction titles under one pen name (visible on any Amazon author page)
- People asking tool questions in r/selfpublish and the KDP Facebook groups
- Commenters on KDP YouTube channels — they self-identify by asking operator questions
- Anyone who has published a "my KDP results" post anywhere in the last 6 months

### The email

> **Subject:** the pen name mistake that cost me my KDP account
>
> Hi {first name},
>
> I ran a KDP catalog to €400/month and then lost the whole account overnight — over a pen
> name that read like a joke. Every book went with it.
>
> I wrote up the loop I use now so nobody else does the same thing: how I pick niches on
> demand signals, the metadata that actually decides ranking, and the pre-upload checklist
> that keeps the account alive.
>
> Want me to send it? It's four pages, no signup.
>
> Antoine
> drafttodone.io

### Rules

- **One ask, and it is not a demo.** Reply "yes" and you send a link. That is the whole flow.
- **Do not mention the product in email one.** It goes in the playbook, at step 2, inside the method.
- Once a version gets replies, **stop editing it.** The only ongoing job is adding leads.
- Volume beats cleverness. If 200 emails produce zero replies, the offer is wrong — that is
  the signal you are paying for, and it arrives in days rather than months.

## Play 4 — GEO placement off-site (72-hour win)

The site already ranks. What is missing is the **off-site** half — Florian ranked #1 for a
commercial term in 48–72 hours with nothing but a Medium listicle and a LinkedIn article.

Publish the same listicle in three places, niched to the ICP rather than the head term:

- **Medium** — *"The 7 best AI book generators for KDP catalog operators (2026)"*
- **LinkedIn** — the same piece, published as an **article**, not a post. LinkedIn articles are
  indexed by Google, and answer engines read Google.
- **Dev/indie directories** where the audience overlaps.

Make it an honest comparison. List real competitors with real strengths, put DraftToDone where
it genuinely belongs, and say plainly who should not use it. A listicle that only praises one
tool converts nobody and gets discounted by the models anyway.

**Then reverse-engineer the citations.** Ask Perplexity and ChatGPT *"best AI book generator
for KDP"*, open the sources panel, and list every page they cite. Email each one:

> Hi — I saw your roundup of AI book tools. I run DraftToDone, which generates the whole KDP
> package (manuscript, wrap cover, metadata, pen name) rather than just text. Happy to give
> you a free account to try it, and I'm glad to feature your piece on our side too if it's
> useful to our readers.

Some will want money. Some will want a link back. Beginner blogs with a low DR almost always
say yes — and per Tanya, domain rating barely matters for AI visibility.

**Also do the video.** One YouTube video titled with the exact query, thumbnail carrying the
keyword plus the logo, is currently one of the fastest AI-visibility wins available, because
Google and ChatGPT are both surfacing video and almost nobody in this niche has made it.

## Play 5 — The problem-aware video

Marcus's format, which he says has produced seven figures across his own product and affiliate
offers. YouTube is the highest-converting channel in the whole corpus.

**The mistake to avoid:** a solution-aware video (*"how to use DraftToDone"*) only reaches
people who already know you exist. At zero brand, that is nobody.

### Video 1 — script skeleton

**Title:** *"Your KDP books aren't selling. It isn't the algorithm."*

1. **The problem, in their words.** You published six books. A handful of sales, then flat.
   You assume Amazon buried you.
2. **The perspective shift** — verifiable, and it must reframe the problem.
   Amazon is showing the book. The impressions are there. What is missing is that the metadata
   never matched what a buyer types, so the impressions go to the wrong people and the
   click-through tells Amazon to stop. *Walk them into their own KDP dashboard to check.*
   They now believe you know something they did not.
3. **The method, four steps** — the playbook's loop: niche on demand signals → metadata →
   compliance → production as a loop.
4. **The product inside step 2 and 4**, used, not pitched. "Here's the metadata this generates
   from one brief." Let the method do the selling.
5. **CTA to the playbook**, not to pricing.

Production quality is close to irrelevant. Marcus runs the second-largest YouTube education
channel in the world on a webcam, a $100 mic and a $50 light. Retention and idea are everything.

### Videos 2 and 3 — same format, different problem

- *"I published 40 AI books on KDP. Here's what actually happened."*
- *"The 3 things that get a KDP account banned in 2026."*

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | Block |
|-----|-------|
| Mon | Free generations from the weekend's comments + the customer question |
| Tue | Reddit post 1 · outreach batch (add leads, reply to replies) |
| Wed | Record and ship one video |
| Thu | Reddit post 2 · outreach batch |
| Fri | Free generations · log the week's numbers |
| Sat | Reddit post 3 · one off-site listicle or one backlink-exchange batch |

Time-block it. Rob's team blocks whole days for content and lets nothing move them.

---

## What gets logged, weekly

Six numbers, in one sheet, nothing else:

`paying customers · MRR · email list · customer conversations · free generations delivered ·
replies per 100 outreach emails`

The last one is the fastest signal you own. If it stays at zero across two hundred sends, the
offer is wrong — and finding that out in week two is worth more than any of the other five.

---

## The kill criteria

Set them now, before the sunk cost accumulates.

- **Outreach:** 500 sends, <2% reply → the offer is wrong. Change the offer, not the volume.
- **Free generations:** 30 delivered, nobody asks what it costs → the output is not good
  enough yet. Stop selling and go fix it. Gene's line applies: *"If you think your problem is
  distribution, rethink it. Maybe you need a better product."*
- **The list:** 60 days, under 200 signups → the lead magnet is not wanted. Rewrite it once, then
  stop. Rob: *"the biggest L is continuing with a project that isn't validated."*
