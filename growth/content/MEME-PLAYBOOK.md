# The meme play

David Park ($10M/year, Jenny) on where his edge actually comes from: **import tactics from
outside your own niche.** They ran UGC for a SaaS three years before other SaaS founders did,
because it was already standard in e-commerce. Everyone in your niche reads the same blogs and
therefore produces the same playbook.

He names meme accounts specifically — a European compliance VC account he described as the
funniest marketing he'd seen, for a company selling enterprise compliance software. His point:
*"unique ways to attract customers should be the main thing."*

**Nobody is doing memes in the KDP niche.** The content there is uniformly earnest: tutorials,
income reports, "5 tips for your book description." That is the opening.

---

## Why this niche is unusually good for it

Memes work when a group shares a specific, unspoken pain. KDP operators share several, and none
of them are being said out loud:

- **The ban.** Everyone is quietly terrified of it and nobody talks about it until it happens.
- **Amazon's opacity.** No explanation, no appeal, no human.
- **The flat sales graph.** Universal, humiliating, never posted.
- **The gap between the gurus and reality.** "I made $10k in my first month" vs your four sales.
- **The 3am pen-name panic.** Very specific, very shared, never articulated.

Shared pain that nobody says out loud is the exact raw material for a meme account. And you have
the one credential that matters here: **you actually got banned.** You are allowed to joke about
it. Nobody else is.

---

## The account

**Separate from your personal account.** Per Jedi's fresh-account approach: a dedicated handle,
posting one thing consistently, so the algorithm learns exactly who to show it to.

- **Handle:** something that names the pain, not the product. `@kdpbanned`, `@kdppain`,
  `@publisherproblems`. Not `@drafttodone_memes`.
- **Bio:** one line, funny, with the product mentioned as an aside rather than a pitch.
  *"Publishing books on Amazon until Amazon decides otherwise. Rebuilt at drafttodone.io"*
- **Profile picture:** something visual, not a logo. A gravestone, a rejected-stamp, a flat line.
- **Platforms:** X first (memes travel furthest and fastest there), Instagram second.

**Warm it the same way as any account:** search what KDP operators search, engage with their
posts for a few days, then start posting.

## The rules that decide whether this works

**1. Funny first, product barely there.** If it reads as an ad it dies. The product goes in the
bio and occasionally the first comment. Not in the image.

**2. Only joke about pain you have actually had.** You got banned, so ban jokes are yours. You
have a flat sales graph somewhere, so post it. Memes about a pain you have not felt read as
false, and this audience will spot it instantly.

**3. Never punch down at the audience.** Joking about *"idiots publishing AI slop"* insults the
people you are selling to. Joke about **Amazon**, about **the situation**, and about **yourself**.
Self-deprecation is the safe direction and the funniest one.

**4. Post the meme, then be useful in the replies.** Someone will ask a real question under a
meme about pen names. That answer is the actual conversion moment.

**5. Volume beats perfection.** Most memes flop. That is normal — Jedi's point is that volume
exists to give you surface area to find what works. Post daily, keep the two that land, make
more of those.

**6. Do not force a cadence you cannot sustain.** Three genuinely funny memes a week beats
fourteen mediocre ones, because a queue of unfunny memes teaches the algorithm your account is
low value.

## Making them

Templates: **imgflip** or **Canva** for the classic formats. Nano Banana / Gemini for anything
that needs an original image. Keep a folder of the formats that worked so you are not starting
from a blank page each week.

**Screenshot memes are the best ones and take no design at all.** A real Amazon rejection email
with a caption. A real flat sales graph. A real 1-star review. Your actual termination notice.
These outperform template memes consistently in every niche, because they are true.

## Scheduling and measurement

Postiz handles the queue — add the meme account as a separate channel so its performance is
tracked separately from the main one.

**The number that matters is not likes. It is profile clicks.** A meme with 50k views and no
profile clicks reached people who will never buy. A meme with 2k views and 100 profile clicks
found your ICP.

Attribution is live now, so tag the bio link:

```
https://drafttodone.io/en/kdp-operator-playbook?utm_source=memes&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=x_memes
```

Then check the admin attribution table. If `memes` shows signups and no payers after a month, it
is entertainment, not distribution — and you close it, per the kill criteria in
[`../CADENCE.md`](../CADENCE.md).

## The honest risk

Meme accounts are the highest-variance play in the whole plan. They either compound fast or do
nothing at all, and there is no middle.

Treat it as **one of six channels, not the main one.** The free-generation offer and cold
outreach are what produce the first ten customers. Memes are the cheap lottery ticket you buy
while doing the reliable work — and per Sleek, virality without ICP fit converts at close to
zero, so a viral meme that reaches the wrong people is not a win.
