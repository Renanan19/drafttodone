# YouTube scripts — the problem-aware format

YouTube is the highest-converting channel in the whole corpus. Marcus's format has produced
seven figures across his own product and affiliate offers, and Elston built TinyHost to $1M/year
largely on faceless tutorials.

**The mistake to avoid:** solution-aware video (*"how to use DraftToDone"*) only reaches people
who already know the category and already know you. At zero brand awareness that is nobody.

**Production reality check:** Marcus runs the second-largest YouTube education channel in the
world on a webcam, a $100 mic and a $50 light. Retention and idea are everything; production is
almost irrelevant. Do not buy equipment before you have published ten videos.

**The structure, every time:**

1. **The problem, in their words** — no intro, no "hey guys", straight in
2. **The perspective shift** — a verifiable insight that reframes the problem and proves you
   know something they don't
3. **The method** — multi-step, with the product used naturally inside one step
4. **Let the method sell** — by the end they should have convinced themselves
5. **CTA to the playbook**, not to pricing

**Thumbnail:** Tanya's finding is that a thumbnail carrying the exact keyword plus your logo is
currently one of the fastest AI-visibility wins available, because Google and ChatGPT both
surface video and almost nobody in this niche has made any.

## Video 1 — "Your KDP books aren't selling. It isn't the algorithm."

The flagship. Publish this one first.

**Title:** Your KDP Books Aren't Selling. It Isn't The Algorithm.
**Thumbnail:** a KDP dashboard with the impressions column circled. Text: "IT'S NOT THE ALGO"

### [0:00–0:40] The problem

> You published six books. Maybe ten. The first few sold a handful of copies to people who
> probably know you, and then it went flat.
>
> You've read that Amazon buries new authors. You've read that you need reviews before the
> algorithm shows you to anyone. So you're sitting on a catalog that took real work and real
> money, waiting for something to change.
>
> I want to show you something in your own dashboard that changes how you read this, and it'll
> take about ninety seconds.

### [0:40–3:00] The perspective shift

> Open KDP. Go to your reports. Find impressions.
>
> [Screen recording — do this live, on a real account]
>
> Here's a book that sold four copies last month. And here's its impression count: several
> thousand.
>
> Amazon showed this book to thousands of people. It was not buried. It was not suppressed. It
> was shown, several thousand times, and almost everyone who saw it scrolled past.
>
> That's a completely different problem, and it's much better news, because suppression isn't
> fixable and packaging is.
>
> Here's the part that matters: when a large share of people who see your book don't click,
> Amazon learns that this book doesn't satisfy this search. So it shows it less. The flatline
> you're looking at isn't the algorithm deciding against you at the start — it's the algorithm
> responding to a click-through rate your metadata earned.
>
> So the question was never "how do I get Amazon to show my book." It's already showing it. The
> question is what happens in the second and a half between someone seeing it and scrolling past.

### [3:00–9:00] The method

**Step 1 — Find out what they actually typed.**

> Your metadata is written in your words. Buyers search in theirs.
>
> Open Amazon, start typing the job your book solves, and read the autocomplete. That's real
> search behaviour from real buyers, updated continuously, and it's free.
>
> [Screen recording: autocomplete for a real niche]
>
> Write down every phrase that matches what your book actually does. Not what you wish it did.

**Step 2 — Split the work between title and subtitle.**

> Most operators make the title do two jobs: carry the promise and carry the keywords. It does
> neither well.
>
> The title carries the promise. The subtitle carries the search phrase.
>
> "Sourdough Baking" is a topic. "Why Your Sourdough Is Flat" is what somebody typed at eleven
> at night after their fourth failure.
>
> [Show three real before/after examples]

**Step 3 — Use all seven slots, and use them as phrases.**

> Seven slots. Most people fill three, and fill them with single words.
>
> "sourdough" is a word. "why is my sourdough flat" is a phrase somebody typed.
>
> And if a word is already in your title, it's indexed. Repeating it in a slot buys you nothing
> and burns a slot you can't get back.
>
> [Here is where the product appears, used, not pitched]
>
> This is the part I automated, because it's mechanical. I give it a niche brief and it produces
> the title, subtitle, description and all seven slots along with the manuscript and cover.
> [10 seconds of the actual output on screen]
> But run this by hand for your first few — you need to develop the judgement before you delegate it.

**Step 4 — Fix the first two lines of the description.**

> Most buyers read two lines and decide.
>
> So the outcome goes in those two lines. Not your credentials. Nobody has decided to care about
> you yet.

**Step 5 — Change one thing and wait 30 days.**

> Change only the subtitle. Leave everything else. Wait thirty days and compare.
>
> Change five things at once and you'll learn nothing, which is what most people do and why
> everyone has a theory and nobody has data.

### [9:00–10:00] Close

> None of this needs a new book. It's the catalog you already have.
>
> I've put the whole loop — niche selection, metadata, the compliance checklist that keeps your
> account alive — into a free playbook. No signup: drafttodone.io/en/kdp-operator-playbook
>
> If you try the subtitle test, come back and tell me what happened. I'll read it.

## Video 2 — "I published AI books on KDP. Here's what actually happened."

**Title:** I Published AI-Generated Books On KDP. Here's What Actually Happened.
**Thumbnail:** a stack of books, one marked with a red X. Text: "THE HONEST NUMBERS"

**Angle:** radical honesty, including the failures. This is the video that makes every other
video you make believable.

- **[0:00]** Problem: everyone's either selling you a dream or telling you it's all slop.
- **[0:30]** Perspective shift: the real constraint isn't generation speed, it's your ability to
  review each book properly. Show the maths — what N books actually cost once your own hours are
  counted, and the break-even number that falls out of it.
- **[2:00]** Method — what worked, what didn't, honestly:
  - The six failure modes in long generated manuscripts (the Reddit teardown, on video)
  - How long the fixes actually take
  - What KDP accepted and what it rejected
  - Which books sold and which didn't, with the sheet on screen
- **[8:00]** The rule: publish fewer than you can technically produce. The constraint is review
  capacity, not generation.
- **[9:00]** CTA to the checklist.

## Video 3 — "The 3 things that get a KDP account banned in 2026"

**Title:** The 3 Things That Get A KDP Account Banned In 2026
**Thumbnail:** your own termination email, redacted. Text: "THIS HAPPENED TO ME"

**Angle:** the highest-stakes topic in the niche, told by someone it happened to. This is the
one most likely to travel.

- **[0:00]** Cold open on the termination email. "This is mine. €400 a month, gone overnight."
- **[0:45]** Perspective shift: termination is **account-level**, not title-level. Most people
  plan as though a bad book costs them that book. It can cost the catalog.
- **[2:00]** The three, in the order operators actually hit them:
  1. Undisclosed AI content — and the distinction KDP draws between generated and assisted
  2. Exceeding the daily title limit — why bursts look like exactly what the system hunts
  3. A pen name that reads as misleading — **your story**, in full
- **[7:00]** What is *not* on the list: using AI. Declaring it is not a penalty. Not declaring
  it is.
- **[8:00]** The checklist as the fix. CTA.

## Tutorial videos — the Elston play, run in parallel

Faceless, evergreen, jobs-to-be-done. Some of his took a year to rank and then did 100K views.
Nobody searches your category; they search the job.

Make one for each, screen recording, no face, 5–8 minutes:

- How to make a KDP cover (front, spine and back)
- How to write a book description that actually sells
- How to choose your 7 KDP keywords
- How to calculate KDP royalties before you set a price
- How to pick a KDP category you can actually rank in
- How to set up a pen name properly
- How to format a manuscript for KDP paperback

Each one ends with the same line, pointing at the free tool on the site that does that job.
These are the videos that will still be earning in two years.

## Publishing rhythm

| | |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–4 | One flagship video per week (1, 2, 3, then the first tutorial) |
| Weeks 5+ | One flagship + one tutorial per week |
| Every video | Cut 3–5 vertical clips for X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Shorts |

Consistency beats production. Ten videos before you judge whether the format works — five is
not enough data, and twenty in a format that isn't working is stubbornness rather than persistence.
