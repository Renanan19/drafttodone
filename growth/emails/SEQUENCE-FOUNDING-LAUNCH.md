# Sequence 2 — the Founding Operator launch

The scarcity sequence. This is Rob Hoffman's step 4, and it is the mechanic that turned a
3,624-person waitlist into ~$20K MRR in one month for Mentions.

**Do not run this until the list is at 300+.** Below that the scarcity is not credible and you
burn the mechanic — you only get to launch a cohort once.

**Seat count:** 50 total. Update `FOUNDING_SEATS_CLAIMED` in `app/home-content.ts` as they fill,
so the site and the emails never disagree.

**The offer — access, not discount.** The algorithm is not discounted, so the founding cohort
gives *more* rather than *cheaper*: a direct line to Antoine, roadmap priority, and the kit.
Nadav held his price for a year while people asked for a cheaper tier, refused, and it worked —
and his rule is that you can always improve terms later but never reduce them. A founding price
would spend that option on day one.

**The proof that carries the launch:** two books this system produced reached #1 in their Amazon
category. Never name the books or the pen names. Say "category", not "bestseller" — operators
know the difference and claiming more than the fact makes the fact worthless.

## Email 3 — objection handling (day 4)

**Subject:** [N] left — the three questions I keep getting

> [N] of 50.
>
> Three questions, answered straight.
>
> **"Is this allowed on KDP?"**
> Yes, with disclosure. KDP asks you to declare AI-generated content at upload and distinguishes

**Running the call — the five things that make it work:**

1. An offer only available to attendees (a 1:1 catalog review is the natural one here).
2. **Cap it.** "The first 5 people who take a seat on this call get the review."
3. **Announce each purchase live.** FOMO and social proof at once.
4. The live build is the meat. This product is inherently demo-able — most SaaS isn't. Use it.
5. **Bring a real customer on.** Interview them. This is the single highest-converting element,
   and you have two customers — ask both.

## After it closes

**Close it.** The mechanic only works if the deadline is real. If you extend, nobody believes
the next one.

Then, per Rob's step 6: **talk to all 50.** Record every call, feed the transcripts to an LLM,
pull out the most-requested features, the bugs and the exact language they use. Their words
become the landing page copy and the roadmap.

Then repeat the cycle: cohort 2 at a smaller discount, then the public launch. Three launches,
three scarcity events, three waves. Rob did exactly this to $60K MRR in 53 days.
