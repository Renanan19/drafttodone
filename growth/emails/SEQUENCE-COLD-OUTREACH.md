# Sequence 3 — cold outreach

Roman: *"Outreach is number one, because you know right away if it works. Send 5,000 emails and
get no answers — your offer is wrong."* Nothing else gives a signal that fast.

**Two rules that govern everything below:**

1. **Never ask for a demo. Offer the blueprint.** Saying yes to a document is easy. Most people
   never book a call — they go start a trial instead. Roman later told Florian this was his
   single most profitable channel.
2. **Once a version gets replies, stop editing it.** The only ongoing job is adding leads and
   answering.

**Technical, and not optional:** send from a **separate warmed domain**, never
`drafttodone.io`. Set SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Warm it for two weeks before the first send. Founders
routinely discover 20% of their mail was going to spam and had no idea.

## Sequence A — KDP operators

**Who:** authors with 3+ non-fiction titles under one pen name, people asking tool questions in
KDP communities, anyone who posted their KDP results in the last six months.

**Volume:** start at 30/day from one domain. Scale only after the reply rate is known.

> the account alive.

>
> Antoine

**Subject:** it isn't the algorithm

### Email 4 — day 12

**Subject:** want me to generate one for free?

> Hi {first_name},
>
> Last one from me.

> Reply with a niche and I'll generate a complete KDP package — manuscript, full wrap cover,
> title, subtitle, description, all 7 keyword slots, pen name — and send it over. Free, nothing

**Why four and not five:** the fourth is a bigger offer than the first three, so it earns its
place. A fifth adds nothing but complaints.

## Sequence C — backlink and listicle exchange

For the GEO play. Targets: every page ChatGPT and Perplexity already cite when asked *"best AI
book generator"*. Open the sources panel, list them, email every one.

### Email 1

**Subject:** your AI book tools roundup

> Hi {name},
>
> I saw your roundup of AI book tools — ChatGPT cites it when people ask about this category,
> which is how I found it.
>
> I run DraftToDone. It's a bit different from most of the list: it generates the whole KDP
> package — manuscript, wrap cover, metadata, pen name — rather than just text.
>
> Happy to give you a free account to try it, no obligation to include it. And if it's useful
> to your readers, I'm glad to feature your piece on our side too.
>
> Either way, good roundup.
>
> Antoine

**What comes back:** some want money (€100–200 is normal for this and is often worth it), some
want a reciprocal link, and beginner blogs with a low domain rating almost always say yes.
Per Tanya, domain rating barely matters for AI visibility — a brand-new blog with almost no
content can get cited. Email those too.

## What to track

One sheet, five columns: `date · target · sequence · replied · outcome`.

**The only number that matters early is replies per 100 sent.**

- Under 2% after 500 sends → **the offer is wrong.** Change the offer, not the volume. That is
  the signal you are paying for, and it arrives in days rather than months.
- Above 5% → stop editing, add leads, and answer faster.
