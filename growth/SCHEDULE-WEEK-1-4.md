# The first four weeks, scheduled

Load one week at a time into Postiz on Sunday. Everything referenced here is already written in
[`content/`](./content/) — this file only decides **what goes out when, and where**.

**Posting times** are Europe/Paris. The X and LinkedIn slots target the US morning, because most
of this audience is American. Adjust once you have real data, not before.

| Slot | Time | Platform |
|---|---|---|
| A | 08:30 | LinkedIn |
| B | 15:00 | X |
| C | 17:00 | Reddit (manual for the first two weeks) |

**One rule that overrides the schedule:** if a post gets real engagement, reply to every comment
within the hour. That matters more than the next scheduled post.

## Reruns are fine

Julia's point: *"Don't say 'I already posted this.' Post the same messaging until it stops
working."* A post that performed will perform again a month later with a changed opening — a
tiny fraction of your audience saw it the first time.

Track which posts performed. From week 5 the schedule stops being this file and starts being
**more of whatever worked**, which is the entire point of the volume.

---

## What this schedule deliberately does not do

- **No posting on Sunday.** The cadence has to survive twelve weeks.
- **No Reddit automation for the first two weeks.** Post those by hand from a warmed account.
  Reddit is unusually good at spotting scheduled promotion, and a banned account costs the
  channel permanently.
- **No Instagram or TikTok.** Not the play for a product-led prosumer SaaS.
- **No engagement bait.** It reaches people who will never buy.
