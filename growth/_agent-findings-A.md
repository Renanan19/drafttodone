## 1. "Lotz" — sold 4 mobile apps for $500K (Bible Buddy, Magic Music, PrayScreen); now YC

- **Validate with 3 fake Figma screens + UGC, before writing any code.** Pay a designer $50–100 for three screens of a *shareable asset* inside the app, then film content that makes it look like a working product. Nobody demos every screen — they show 1–3. You are testing content-market fit, not the product. Cheap pre-build test for DraftToDone: three mockup screens of "brief in → full KDP package out" and see whether the hook lands.
- **The signal threshold is 5,000–10,000 views CONSISTENTLY, not virality.** His actual PrayScreen sequence: day 1 = 1,000 views, day 2 = 900, day 3 = 4,000 → kept repeating the same hook/visual → 80K → 100K → only then ran paid ads on that exact creative → $0.50–0.60 cost per install. "Virality is a black swan; you can't calculate it."
- **[CONTRADICTS] Fix acquisition cost before touching the paywall.** App Store average free→paid is **2.7%**. If you're already at 5%, stop optimizing conversion — it's above benchmark. The lever is halving cost-per-install, which doubles revenue at the same conversion rate. Kill thresholds: <$1 CPI = amazing, $1–1.50 = good, $1.50–2 = fixable, >$2 = kill the idea.
- **Spend 90% of build time on onboarding, not the app.** Ad's only job = signup. Onboarding's only job = free→paid. Product's only job = retention. Track **completion rate** (% who reach the end of onboarding); **85% is the absolute worst acceptable**. Find the specific screen causing drop-off.
- **Move every permission/friction ask to AFTER payment.** Concrete: PrayScreen's Screen Time permission request in onboarding was killing signups → moved it to post-payment. A gender question with an "other" option caused a **~30% drop-off on one screen** → deleted the screen. Direct read-across: don't ask a KDP operator for their niche research / KDP account details / preferences before they've paid.
- **Fear beats love as an emotion in the hook.** His zero-budget content plan was built explicitly on fear of loss, not aspiration. For DraftToDone the fear is pre-loaded: the founder's own permanent KDP ban over a joke pen name. That is the strongest asset in the whole marketing plan and it's currently underused.
- **The shareable artifact IS the distribution.** Magic Music: users shared the *song*, which opened a web view where the song played free with a big green "create your own" button → download → they share again. Nobody shares apps; they share outputs. Read-across: publish generated covers/blurbs on public shareable pages with "generate your own cover" as the only CTA — the cover, not the SaaS, is the viral object.
- **Ops hygiene from day one: one dedicated `info@yourdomain` email for every single third-party login** (Stripe, analytics, hosting, LLM APIs). He's been burned repeatedly; it is the single longest step in any acquisition transfer. Zero-cost to do now, expensive to retrofit.
- **Repost 10 creator TikToks onto ONE branded Instagram account, then run ads on that post** (not on a raw asset) — it looks like an organic post, carries comments, and the profile looks alive, which lifts ad→install conversion.
- Exit math if it ever matters: buyers pay **2–3× yearly profit**; sell when ~40% of the growth curve is still ahead; take the 2nd or 3rd best offer (optimize for speed of close, not price — the delta between $250K and $300K is noise); never be a buyer's first acquisition.

## 2. Nick — Sideshift ($8M ARR UGC marketplace)

- **Idea/gap mining: read the 1-star reviews of the incumbents.** "Look for products already doing extremely well, read their app store reviews, find where the fallacies lie." **You don't need 100% better — 25% better is enough.** Immediately actionable: mine 1–2 star reviews and refund threads for Publisher Rocket, Atticus, BookBolt, KDSPY, Sudowrite, Designrr. That is a free, concrete BOFU content + roadmap source.
- **Do the content yourself first, at volume, before paying anyone.** His own reps: **4 posts/day across 5 accounts = 20+ posts/day**; first viral video (5M views) after **3 weeks** from zero followers. You cannot direct creators until you've been one.
- **[CONTRADICTS] "creator co-founder / hire creators in your niche."** He says niche-expert creators are the single biggest mistake brands make. You want **micro-actors who understand the algorithm** — good lighting, readable captions, will ship 60 videos — not KDP experts. "Brad Pitt wasn't cast in F1 because he's a race car driver."
- **[CONTRADICTS] build-in-public.** "Build in public is great for brand awareness but won't drive conversion for a consumer/prosumer product. In true early days, pick your battles: validate product, get PMF, get early customers." He only endorses personal brand for B2B where inbound comes from referrals.
- **Brand-new account per product, then WARM IT UP before posting.** Before posting anything for a calorie-counter app, spend time searching and engaging with fitness content so the algorithm classifies the account and distributes to the right demographic. Do NOT post product content from the personal account.
- **Concrete UGC test budget: $1,500–2,000.** 4 creators × $500 base → 30–60 videos each → **120+ pieces of content**. "If you can't discern anything after 120 pieces, go back to the drawing board." Success signal is not a viral video — it's a hook that did 5,000–6,000 views instead of nothing.
- Volume model at scale: 12 creators × 30–60 posts each on a dedicated account; **300 videos/month, ~10 go viral** — treated explicitly as a VC portfolio.
- Ops: every customer gets an individualized Slack channel and the founder's personal phone number. 800 customers, **inbound is 80% of sales**. Free trial is 7 days.
- Creator management, once you have any: leaderboards, merch, public shout-outs. Camaraderie is what turns a good program into a great one.

## 3. Tibo (Thibault) — Tweet Hunter/Taplio ($8M exit); now Outrank $200K/mo, SuperX, Revid

- **THE FLYWHEEL WORTH COPYING: the micro-equity influencer syndicate.** He gave **15 Twitter influencers 0.1% each of profits AND 0.1% of the exit**, with **zero contractual obligation** to post. Result: **~80% of people he approached said yes**, and every feature launch got amplified by 15 high-caliber accounts. He did this at only ~$30K MRR — you don't need to be the market leader, you need to make it trivially easy to say yes. Direct translation: 10–15 KDP YouTubers / newsletter writers / Facebook group admins at 0.1% each, no obligations. Far cheaper and more repeatable than one creator co-founder.
- Separately, the single big influencer partner got **25% equity**, ran his own launch, and took MRR **from $3K to $20K in two weeks** (Sept 2021), then **20% MoM for 18 months → $200K/mo**. The anticipated post-launch churn crash never happened.
- **[CONTRADICTS feature marketing] Sell the outcome two levels up, not the feature.** Tweet Hunter's copy went from "get inspiration for better content" (surface, feature-based) to "better content → more followers → better brand → **more leads, more sales, more money**". "It worked 10 times more." For DraftToDone: stop selling "manuscript + cover + 7 keywords" and sell "more listed titles → more royalty months → no compliance strike."
- **Wire the in-app support button directly to your personal X/DM inbox until $10K MRR.** He did this for every product. The point isn't bug reports — it's learning the customer's *core workflow* and whether your product sits inside it.
- **THE RETENTION FIX FOR A WEEKLY SUBSCRIPTION: invert who does the work.** Outrank started as a blog-post generator you had to log in and use — nobody has that discipline, so churn was high. They flipped it: **the software produces and publishes automatically every day whether you show up or not; you can review before it goes out, but if you don't, it ships anyway.** That forcing function is what pulled users back in and "drastically improved retention and reduced churn." This is the highest-value idea in the batch for a €14.99/week product: a KDP operator paying weekly needs a *book package produced every week on a schedule*, not a tool they must remember to open.
- Validation cadence before scaling: work closely with **10–20 users for a few weeks/months** to find what makes it sticky, *then* go broad with audience/newsletter/SEO/ads.
- Exit terms to avoid: their deal was **25% cash up front, 75% earnout over 2 years** tied to revenue milestones — and because they insisted on keeping control, the buyer never helped with promotion. His rule now: **no earnout, 3-month transition max.**
- Network-effect SEO idea: Outrank **cross-links its own customers' sites to each other** to build authority inside the user base. Breaks down for isolated niches. Only relevant to DraftToDone if there's ever an author/book directory.
- (Sponsor read, treat with care) He/Florian claim 260 backlinks bought from Linkuma at ~$5 each. Paid link buying conflicts with the AEO/GEO + no-fake-trust posture already documented for other Antoine projects. Noted, not recommended.

## 4. Yasser — Chatbase (0 → $1M ARR in 117 days, now $8M ARR)

- **Channel partnership = "do sales to one entity, get 1,000 customers."** Shopify app + Vercel marketplace integration (buy Chatbase from inside Vercel's dashboard). "You need something big to get your foot in the door in a new vertical; then PLG takes over." KDP has no app store — so the equivalent entities are the aggregators of KDP operators: Kindlepreneur/Publisher Rocket's list, 20BooksTo50K, Self Publishing Formula, the big KDP Facebook group admins, Book Bolt. One partnership > 1,000 cold DMs.
- **Vertical pods instead of one generic message.** Don't market "AI for KDP" generically; run several parallel mini go-to-markets — low-content journals, kids' activity books, devotionals, cookbooks, workbooks — double down on whichever converts, keep small experiments running in the others.
- **[CONTRADICTS churn-flow optimization] Churn is 60–70% just "the product is good."** He took churn from **27% → 8.8%**, target 3%. Cancellation flows, dunning, revenue recovery, card handling = "the last 20%, insignificant" until you're at or below average churn. Don't build a save-flow at €0 MRR; build the product.
- **Organic content early is a multiplier on every other channel, not a channel.** It makes cold email not-cold, makes paid ads convert (brand recognition), and builds the domain authority/dwell time that makes SEO work. Sequence he endorses: organic/viral first for the boost → then SEO, word of mouth, paid, outbound for linear, reliable growth.
- **Decay warning on the known B2B-influencer playbook:** in 2023/early-2024 one LinkedIn post with a screen recording getting 5,000 likes moved **Stripe MRR by +$5,000** with nothing else explaining it. He explicitly says **this stopped working** — now it requires far more effort per piece and "hey can you talk about us" is dead.
- **Never outsource anything critical to growth to an agency.** "Caring is more important than experience." Agencies only for experimental/side initiatives. He worked with many; most were bad, and the mistake was giving them the critical work.
- Tooling for PLG: watch customers screen-share while using the product, plus PostHog/Amplitude + session recordings — but the *starting point* must come from first-principles thinking about who the customer is, not from the data.

## 5. Rob — SuperX ($1.2K → $14K/mo in ~6 months)

- **THE MAX-MRR FORMULA (most important number in the batch): Max MRR = monthly new revenue ÷ churn rate.** His live math: 15 trial signups/day × 30% conversion = ~$5,265/mo new revenue; ÷ 0.30 churn = **$17.5K ceiling — no matter how much marketing he does**. Same signups with churn at 5% = **$105K/mo**. Same signups at 30/day with 10% churn = $100K/mo. For DraftToDone at €14.99/**week**, churn is the entire business model; compute this ceiling before spending a euro on acquisition.
- **[CONTRADICTS launch-day / go-viral-now] Don't spend viral attention while churn is high.** "I feel like I have an energy meter I can use to go viral and get signups. I don't want to expend it right now — they'd churn. So we're doing passive signups until churn is down." At €0 MRR with 2 customers, this argues for fixing the retention loop *before* the waitlist→beta→launch sequence already planned.
- **Post-viral churn wave lands ~45 days later.** His actual arc: $1.2K → $2.5K in 2 days at launch → $6K over 45 days → **churn pullback to ~$6–7K over the next 45 days** → creep to $8K → $10K → viral "$10K MRR" post → $13K → $14K. Plan for the pullback; it is not a failure signal.
- **The gym-membership vs. personal-trainer diagnosis.** Churning users weren't seeing value fast enough. SuperX was a toolkit (AI writer, analytics, engage) — "a gym membership where you don't know how to use the machines." Fix = an in-product **coach** that says *do this today*, then *here's the result you got yesterday, now do this*. Exactly the DraftToDone problem: a KDP operator handed a generator will churn; one handed "this week's title, this week's niche, here's what last week's book did" won't.
- **The 4-post content loop (X):** entertaining → educational → convincing/selling → inspirational → repeat. Mechanic: a viral *entertaining* post "warms you up in the algorithm" — X then serves your next posts to everyone who engaged, so the *educational* post rides that warmed audience, the sell sits at the bottom of the value, and the inspirational post is a screenshot of the result which loops back. Bonus if the entertaining and educational posts are thematically linked.
- **X profile setup that compounds:** real photo of your face (the anti-AI-slop signal), 1–2 line bio, and a **pinned post stating a public goal with a number and a promise to document it**, left pinned for a year. His: "My goal is to build a SaaS to $10,000/month and document the process." Everything below the pin is receipts. DraftToDone's version writes itself: banned from KDP over a pen name → building the tool that makes catalog publishing compliant and repeatable → €0 to €X, documented.
- **Minimum Viable Video.** Define in advance the smallest content unit you can ship on your worst day — his was a clip + a song + a caption, no talking. He shipped it from hospital. This is what makes 131 consecutive days possible. He hit $10K/mo on day 131 of daily video, then downshifted to weekly.
- **24–48 hours off the internet is enough for people to forget you.** Fix without burning out: one hour every Monday scheduling seven posts.
- **X algorithm, Dec 2025: media + long raw opinion text, and quote-tweets.** Time-on-platform is the objective function. His own back-to-back numbers on "photo + unfiltered opinion" travel posts: **607K, 574K, 181K, 133K, 73K views**. Rage bait mechanically works and costs you integrity and followers; strongly-held real opinions accidentally do the same job.
- **Viral feature ideation, exactly:** watch for a *concept* that already went viral (e.g. someone's "tweet virality simulator" doing millions of views), then **rebuild it as a feature of your product and launch a video about it** — proven to go viral once, will go again. Evergreen concepts resurface every **2–3 months**; wait 2–3 months, add your own spin, people have forgotten. Also **repackage the same feature in a new video format**: screen recording = 80K views, the dancing version of the same feature = 130K views. And name features after trending topics for borrowed attention ("Cursive for tweets").
- **Affiliate lever:** tweeted publicly at Greg Eisenberg after Dan Koe mentioned SuperX in a video → Greg signed up as an affiliate and put the link in the YouTube description → **~100 signups**. Tactic: publicly thank anyone who mentions you, then immediately convert them into an affiliate.
- **Reward QA-grade feedback with product credit.** One user built a QA spreadsheet with screenshots; Rob gave him generous free credits. "Every friction point is lost revenue."
- **Filter churn feedback by ICP.** Students demanding a discount are not the ICP — "if someone doesn't want to take X seriously, SuperX isn't for them." Signal vs. noise is the whole job. Ruthlessly relevant given DraftToDone's ICP is locked to catalog operators, not novelists.
- **Table stakes before 10x.** Tibo's rule to him: to pull users off Tweet Hunter you must first *match* its boring features (auto-DM), then be 10× better. Read-across: match the boring things a KDP operator already gets from Publisher Rocket/Atticus before selling the magic.
- Paid ads counter-signal: at $14K MRR, Google Ads was only **marginally** profitable and Meta Ads was **ROAS-negative**. Price point $29–39/mo.

## 7. Dmytro — ScreenshotOne ($24.8K MRR, solo, B2B API)

- **ICP = a real named human, not a profile.** "There is no abstract entity that wants to buy your product." Write the landing page, the blog posts and the social posts *to one real person you can name*. Even better: interview them about **how they did the job before your product existed**. His landing page says "Screenshot API **for developers**" with a code snippet — and marketers/PMs/no-coders still convert, because "it's not for me but it does what I need."
- **[Corollary that contradicts endless ICP research] Betting on the WRONG ICP beats having none.** "You need to bet on something. Write something that resonates with that profile. If it doesn't resonate after ~6 months, switch to a different real person and repeat." Faster than trying to be right first.
- **Publish SEO content BEFORE the product exists.** He started writing in **January 2022** and launched in **May 2022** — 5 months of content first, so there was Google traffic on launch day. DraftToDone is pre-revenue right now; this window is open and free.
- **The SEO loop, no tools needed:** write content around the niche → open Google Search Console → see which keywords the post *actually* ranks for (not the ones you targeted) → rewrite the post to match those keywords → repeat until position 1 → then chase backlinks to that page. He estimates **50–60% of revenue** comes from SEO.
- **[CONTRADICTS standard comparison-post SEO] Put real backlinks to your best competitors inside your "best X tools" post.** Reasoning: if a visitor clicks through to a competitor, they don't bounce back to Google and re-search — Google reads search intent as satisfied and you keep the position. Everyone else names competitors without linking. He held #1 for "best screenshot API" for a long time. Applies directly to a DraftToDone "best KDP tools" page.
- Anecdote worth testing: he mentioned unrelated keywords in a social post about a technical article, and Google started ranking the article **for the keywords from the social post**, not the article's own. Social does feed SEO.
- **[CONTRADICTS badge/directory tactics] Paid directories: good traffic, zero conversions → he stopped.** Directories may still be worth it as backlinks, but do not model them as an acquisition channel.
- **Be where the workflow already is:** official Zapier integration (plus Make, n8n, MCP) — customers search "how to automate X", find Zapier, and pull him in as the API. Also sponsors GitHub repos so "built by ScreenshotOne" appears at the top of technical searches.
- **Attribution reality check:** X → (later) branded Google search is invisible; it shows up as "came from Google." He distrusts founders who claim "SEO is my only channel" while holding a large X following. Only *deep, non-branded* page conversions are honest SEO. Tool: PostHog.
- Affiliate program: never actively promoted, still passively produces **$1–2K of $24.8K MRR (~4–8%)**.
- **Niche ceiling warning, worth taking seriously:** "no matter how much marketing energy I invest, I cannot grow past ~$1–3K MRR added per month — the market is small and competitors split it." A locked, narrow ICP buys clarity and caps upside. Sizing the "KDP catalog operator" population is a real pre-spend question.
- **[CONTRADICTS Rob's authenticity advice]** Rob (ep 5) says be vulnerably yourself. Dmytro says: "If you really want to grow, go optimize for the algorithm — sell your soul for a year, then switch to authentic content. Don't lie to yourself: you're doing it for business." His own balanced, nuanced posts get 30 likes / 2,000 views — and still convert roughly one customer each.

## Top 3 things Antoine should actually do

**1. Compute the Max MRR ceiling before spending anything on acquisition, then redesign the product around it.**
Max MRR = (new revenue per month) ÷ churn. At €14.99/week the weekly plan is a churn machine, and Rob's arithmetic proves marketing cannot outrun it. Then apply Tibo's Outrank fix — **invert who does the work**: DraftToDone should produce the next book package on a schedule automatically, and let the operator review before it ships rather than requiring them to log in and start a generation. Pair that with Rob's coach layer ("here's this week's package; here's what last week's title did"). A tool a KDP operator must remember to open will churn at 30%; a system that ships every week won't. Everything else on this list is worthless if the ceiling is €17K.

**2. Build the pinned-post + micro-equity syndicate combo — the two cheapest, highest-leverage moves in the batch.**
(a) Rob's X profile setup: real photo, one-line bio, and a pinned post with a public number and a promise to document. Antoine's hook is already the strongest in the entire dataset — permanently banned from KDP over a joke pen name, now building the compliance-first catalog tool — and it's Lotz's "fear beats love" emotion, pre-loaded and true.
(b) Tibo's syndicate: offer **0.1% of profits and 0.1% of any exit to 10–15 KDP creators** (YouTubers, newsletter writers, big group admins), **with no contractual obligation to post**. ~80% said yes to Tibo at $30K MRR; the ask is easy precisely because it costs them nothing. That is a launch amplifier, a feedback panel and a distribution moat for 1.5% of the company — dramatically cheaper than the creator-co-founder route and it survives any single creator going cold.

**3. Start the pre-launch SEO/content bank now, seeded from competitor 1-star reviews.**
Dmytro published for 5 months before launch and had Google traffic on day one — DraftToDone is pre-revenue, so that window is open right now and costs nothing but time. Seed it with Nick's method: mine 1–2 star reviews of Publisher Rocket, Atticus, BookBolt, KDSPY, Sudowrite for the exact complaints of catalog operators — that output is simultaneously the content calendar, the BOFU comparison pages, and the product roadmap ("25% better beats 100% better"). Include Dmytro's contrarian move on the "best KDP tools" page: **link out to the best competitors for real**, which is also the only version of that page that survives the AEO/GEO no-fake-trust rule. Then run the loop: publish → check Search Console for what actually ranks → rewrite to match → repeat.
