## 1. Rajan Cheetah — 370K IG in ~1 year, building a luxury cabin in public

- **Go viral *inside* the series, not outside it.** He distinguishes two kinds of viral: videos that get views, and videos that get views *and* convert to followers. Memes/funny videos hit millions and convert nobody — "they're not invested." A viral video that sits inside an ongoing serial arc converts because people follow to see the next episode. Rule: never chase a format outside your series just because it did 6M views.
- **The video that broke him open was the *finance* video, not the build video.** His #1 post (6M views, ~80–90K followers) was him talking through his **construction loan** — real numbers on screen + foreshadowing ("this loan is going to build *this*") + it stepped sideways from his core niche (construction) into an adjacent one (finance). Adjacent-niche step-out from inside the series is the pattern. Antoine's equivalent: the *money/compliance* mechanics of a KDP catalog, not the "look what I built" video.
- **Concentration reality: ~half his followers came from 4 videos out of ~180.** Optimize for shots on goal, not average quality. Florin Pop says the same thing in ep. 3: "every piece of content is a lottery ticket."
- **Reposting a winner via Instagram Trial Reels stacked ~2x the followers.** Original post of his best video ≈ 40–50K followers; reposting it through trial reels repeatedly added up to ~80–90K total. Free compounding on an existing winner — no new production.
- **DM every single new commenter, referencing something from their profile.** Not "thanks" — he opens their profile and says "nice cars by the way." Also answers ~90% of DMs and comments manually and does 15-min calls with random followers. Claims 1-to-1 contact with ~6,000 of ~300,000 = **2% of the audience**. Result: 100–150 people auto-comment on every new post from day one of publishing. His stated first goal for a new creator: *"connect one-on-one with the first 20 new people who show up on your page."* This is the single tactic with a guaranteed floor at zero audience.
- **The explicit ask on those calls:** "I don't want money from you. All I ask is that you engage with my stuff when you can." Asking for engagement directly, as a favour, not a CTA.
- **[CONTRADICTS] 700–800 emails collected with zero call to action.** He never told anyone to enter their email; they found the link in bio / the site themselves. He argues those are his *highest-intent* people precisely because they self-selected. This is the opposite of the known "lead-magnet post / CTA under every post" mechanic. See also Gosha (ep. 4) and Alex Aiden (ep. 5) for the two other data points — none of the three used a lead-magnet post.
- **Effort number, for calibration:** 6–7 hours per day on ONE 60-second video at his current level. 2–3 hours/day for his first 20. Compare to Alex Aiden's ~5 hours *per month* (ep. 5) and Yoni's 30 min/day (ep. 6). **[CONTRADICTS]** the known "frequency + serial consistency" line, which doesn't say which end of a 100x effort spread you should sit at. Only the cheap end is compatible with also shipping a SaaS.
- **Don't box yourself into the niche.** 0→30K on sneakers, then a 180° pivot to real estate with negligible churn — because he had deliberately sprinkled non-sneaker videos into the sneaker series, so the through-line was always *him*. Relevant: Antoine can lead with the ban story / KDP and pivot later without resetting.
- **[B2C ONLY]** His refusal of all brand deals to "keep the brand pure," and his stated payoff being a network of 100–150 high-net-worth people rather than revenue. He lives with his parents and is broke. Antoine cannot afford this posture.

## 2. Keegan Shivers — 325K IG in 90 days, house renovation

- **The standing anchor hook, repeated verbatim on every single video.** "30 days ago I bought this property for $267,000…" — same number, same sentence shape, every episode. Two jobs at once: recognition for returning viewers ("oh, this guy again") and orientation for new ones. Florian tested it on 3 of his own videos and they outperformed everything else.
- **The literal script template: hook → backstory → problem → plan → progress → CTA.**
  - Hook: 1–3 sentences, catchy line.
  - Context/recap of what this video is: 1–3 sentences.
  - Main conflict or update since last episode.
  - The plan / the progress.
  - A beat of dry humour.
  - Sign-off.
- **The non-CTA CTA.** Nobody says "follow me." Everyone has a signature destination line: "follow along as we bring this house back to life" (Keegan), "I'm posting every single day, see you tomorrow" (Yoni), "follow me gang" (Rajan), "follow your boy" (Yanni). Antoine needs one line naming the destination, not the action.
- **One shoot day per week, multiple outfit changes, 4–5 videos banked.** He drives 3.5h once a week, brings several outfits so the batch reads as separate days, shoots 4–5, then edits each the day before it posts. Editing: 30–45 min best case, 1.5–2h average. This is the exact workflow that makes a Postiz queue honest.
- **ChatGPT project for scripts, but he flags the output as robotic and is switching to Claude for voice.** He offers his project description publicly. Point: the LLM is for filler/structure, not voice.
- **Two songs, alternated every other video, saved as a CapCut preset.** Kills the music-hunting time sink entirely. Nobody has ever complained; new viewers comment that they *like* the song.
- **Post the video you think is bad.** His self-rated worst recent post did 240,000 views. **[CONTRADICTS]** Rajan's "have a high baseline of quality, don't post memes," in the same podcast series.
- **The metric he watches is 3-second hold rate + average watch time**, not likes/saves/shares. He believes IG rewards raw watch time. His 13.9M-view video accumulated "14 years of watch time."
- **Physical "get loose" shake-out before recording.** His diagnosis of his own early failure: "I was so focused on making something good that it made it bad." He was monotone because he was reciting.
- **Platform asymmetry, hard numbers, same videos on all three: 325K IG / 92K TikTok / 9K YouTube.** Rajan's split is the same shape. Instagram is doing all the work for this format. See "What NOT to copy" — this is a fact about *real estate reels*, not about prosumer SaaS.

## 3. Florin Pop — 200K on X, 172K on YouTube, ~$10K/mo avg

- **The single best transferable idea in the batch: the absurd time-boxed public challenge that produces the content as a byproduct.** He built **10 projects in 10 hours, live on Twitch**, posted each one to X as he finished it, then uploaded a *roughly* edited recording to YouTube. That video did 2.3M views and took his channel **30K → 60K subscribers in one month**. He decided on it during a run and started streaming the same day — no announcement, no prep, he researched the app list live on stream. DraftToDone's literal function is producing a book package on a timer; "10 KDP-ready book packages in 10 hours" is the same format with the product as the engine. Gosha (ep. 4) independently names the absurd-challenge format as one of the last remaining ways to differentiate in build-in-public.
- **Mirror X → Threads with zero incremental work.** Florian grew to ~1,700 on Threads by pushing identical posts through Typefully, never opening the app, never replying, never commenting. He notes Mark Lou and Jack Friks do the same. Also: Threads geo-targets off keywords in the post — say "India" and it distributes to India. Near-free channel for a Postiz user.
- **[CONTRADICTS] Revenue breakdown at ~200K followers.** $155K over ~18 months: $62K SaaS + $50K *selling* that SaaS + $40K sponsorships + $20K course + $12K memberships + $10K consulting + $10K misc. At ~200K followers his SaaS was still only ~$3.4K/mo before he sold it, and sponsorships out-earned it as an ongoing line. His own words: *"it's much easier to make $10,000 from a few pieces of content than to grow a SaaS to $10,000/month."* The audience→SaaS-MRR assumption is weaker than the audience→services assumption.
- **"Unlimited consulting" (borrowed from Daniel Vassallo): one payment, 3 years of open calendar access.** Priced $900. Results: 7 sales ≈ $8,000 in spring, then 4 sales ≈ $3,000. Zero product to build. The feared abuse never happened — people who pay that much value their own time and only call with real questions. He blames weak results on messaging that tries to cover everything he's ever done instead of naming one person and one problem.
- **How a sub-4K-subscriber channel gets a sponsor:** design a specific video around one company's product, then pitch *that video* to them. You can't sell reach, so sell consistency + the back catalogue: "I've posted for months and I'm not stopping; when this channel does blow up, your video is already in the library and YouTube drags old videos up with the channel."
- **Sponsor leverage rule:** inbound sponsors pay whatever you ask and hand over creative control; outbound sponsors are picky and pay less. Expect to discount anything you chase.
- **Sponsorships are increasingly bought for LLM visibility.** Florian's own observation, unprompted: companies pay for repeated mentions across YouTube + blog + X so that LLMs name them as "the best tool for X." Direct tie-in to Antoine's existing AEO/GEO work — the same logic applies to getting *mentioned on other people's channels*, and it reframes creator partnerships as an AEO channel, not a reach channel.
- **Build the content system for the burnout window, not for growth.** He explicitly wants pillars/system so something still ships during the periods where he's creatively dead — which he says arrive predictably and repeatedly. He built 6 content pillars over several days of LLM brainstorming.
- **Hater filter:** no real name + no real profile picture = the opinion doesn't count. Stated as a mental-health protocol, not a moral one.
- **[CONTRADICTS] his biggest stated professional regret is exactly the known playbook's starting point:** he never defined a target audience or their pain points, and says that cost him years. But his *actual* growth came from being in the community and posting whatever. Both his advice and his behaviour are in the file; they disagree.

## 4. Gosha — creative director, 15K YouTube, agency 37

- **Put other people's names and faces in your titles and thumbnails. He calls it by far the easiest way to grow.** Florian discovered Gosha's channel because he recognised *other people* on the thumbnails. Gosha's worst-performing videos were ones where he *did* talk about a known person but packaged it abstractly — the recognisable name/face has to be visible in the packaging, not just in the content. Independently confirmed by Alex Aiden in ep. 5, which makes this the strongest cross-episode signal in the batch.
- **[CONTRADICTS] The CTA test, with numbers.** His $60 Notion template, pitched as an explicit in-video segment ("hey guys, I dropped this, go buy it") → **~5 sales**. One video where the template was simply *visible on screen while he worked on something else*, never mentioned → **20x the sales**, and people DM'd him asking what it was. This directly contradicts "CTA under every post" and sharpens the known "edgy selling" tactic into something more specific: *show the product in use as scenery; let them ask.*
- **[CONTRADICTS] Build-in-public is now a red ocean and POV / "day in the life" packaging is burnt.** His view: for most new creators, build-in-public content is currently a *net negative* for the business because it makes them indistinguishable, and clients come to him saying "people think I'm just copying Daniel." He predicts one more packaging wave then saturation. His filter question before you do it: **"Do I have enough separation from other build-in-public creators — in aesthetic, product, and quality?"** If yes, go 100%. If no, don't post yet. This is the sharpest challenge to the known Loïc/Florian "document the journey" line.
- **Three-tier quality ladder, in order:**
  1. *Entry barrier* — good audio (specifically a mic viewers recognise from big podcasts; he refused to start sit-down videos until he had one) + decent camera + multiple angles. Buys the first 30 seconds.
  2. *Elevated* — cinematic editing, your own typography that evolves, cuts synced to music, deliberate black screens.
  3. *Differentiation* — scripting, narrative, topic selection.
  Cheapest credibility purchase is audio. Do it first.
- **Match the aesthetic to a real budget: ~$500/mo editor + music subscription + a DJI Osmo (~$400) ≈ $600–700/mo** is the realistic floor for "aesthetic" content. Don't benchmark against Hormozi (3 cameras, multiple editors, scripts pre-written by staff) or Daniel Dalen (plane tickets, exotic locations) — you're seeing the output of a payroll.
- **The absurd-challenge format as the differentiation play** (converges with Florin Pop): a public, high-stakes, borderline-unbelievable target documented daily. His example: "new creative studio → $5M in a year." The psychology he names: *"if he can do $5M, I can do $500K."* He also flags the cost — this format only works if the investment behind it is real.
- **Retention on the *work* segment is the real funnel signal.** In client YouTube retention graphs, viewers reliably skip the work/product portion and stay for the lifestyle portion. If people actually watch your work segment, your mid-funnel is functioning. Aesthetic = top, work content = mid, newsletter = bottom (where the buyers are).
- **"Wait for the community to ask you for a product."** Daniel Dalen reached 100K subs with no product and no CTA; the glasses became a product because people kept asking what they were. Sound in principle — but see "What NOT to copy," it is a rich person's timeline.
- **[B2C ONLY] The keyword/mood-board process where the keyword list contains none of your product vocabulary.** 20 Pinterest/Figma mood boards before posting anything; his AI-agency client's keyword list has zero mentions of AI, SaaS, or tech — it's "entrepreneur in Bali," "digital nomad," "remote setups," fashion. This sells the *outcome lifestyle*. It works on consumer aspiration audiences. It will actively repel a KDP catalog operator, who is buying repeatability, metadata control, and not-getting-banned — and who reads Bali-lifestyle imagery as guru-bait.
- **[B2C ONLY] "You need a cast."** A channel where you are always the only person on camera reads as icon-cosplay before you've earned it; Daniel Dalen's supporting characters sometimes get more screen focus than he does. Structurally impossible for a solo founder with no audience — the only cheap version is putting customers/other operators on camera.

## 5. Alex Aiden — exited $1.5M/yr SaaS at 22, 250K cross-platform, 5 figures/mo

- **The name-drop hook, as a fill-in-the-blank template:** *"[Known person] is sitting on a [nine-figure] bag if he just does this one thing"* → then explain the strategic gap, and your worldview is the payload. His actual launch video: *"Iman Gadzhi shut down his agency to go all in on SaaS."* First reel he ever made, 2M views, ~20,000 followers. Mechanic in his words: the name harvests the attention of everyone who follows that person — *"you kind of steal people's followers"* — and the argument converts a slice of them. Antoine's version: known KDP/self-publishing figures, or Amazon KDP policy itself as the "character."
- **The analogy hook, for when nobody knows your category.** In 2023 nobody knew what no-code was, so: *"Have you ever built a website before? You know how you drag and drop the pieces together? You can build software the same way now."* He credits Hormozi with doing this constantly. Antoine needs the one-sentence analogy that makes "one brief → complete book package" click for someone who has only ever done it by hand.
- **Type every hook by funnel stage before writing it — and at the start, everything must be top-of-funnel/awareness.** He decides top/mid/bottom first, *then* picks the hook.
- **He does not script reels at all.** Fix the hook in his head, then let it flow. Scripts only for YouTube. Explicitly says holding yourself to a script is what kills reels.
- **Remix your own winners.** When he wants a video to go viral, he opens his own past viral videos and makes a variation of one. **[CONTRADICTS]** the known Marcus outlier/low-competition research method — Alex says he never studied anyone else's content at all. At minimum: once you have *one* winner, self-remixing beats external research.
- **Batching numbers, the cheapest in the batch:** shoots **twice a month**, producing **14–28 reels + 2 YouTube videos per session**. Scripting the two YouTube videos ≈ 1 hour each; the reels ≈ 30 minutes total because they're unscripted. **≈5 hours a month** sustaining 1–2 posts/day for ~2 years. He scripts on Sundays, records mid-week.
- **The newsletter that actually converted: bio link + one explicit promise, no lead magnet, no funnel.** Gaining ~10K followers/day with no offer, he panicked, spun up Beehiiv that afternoon, put the link in bio and made the bio read *"I'm going to send free tips on how to start a software company."* → ~1,000 subscribers/day, 0→20K fast, ~40K now. Florian's counterpoint from other guests: creators with 300K followers and **400** newsletter subscribers. The difference is the single, specific, stated promise in the bio.
- **The recurring monthly email that booked calls every single time — the most directly portable asset in these six episodes.** Once a month: *"Here's 3–5 software ideas for you to run with."* For each idea: (a) why he picked it, (b) how he'd take it to market, (c) how complicated it is to build. Then one CTA at the bottom: *"if you like any of these and want my team to build it, book a call."* His words: it "would just book calls like crazy" and they closed easily. The DraftToDone translation is exact: **"3–5 KDP niches worth publishing into this month"** — demand signal, competition, metadata angle, how he'd package it — then one CTA to the product.
- **Affiliate is the underrated monetization at small scale.** Make all the information free, affiliate the tools the audience must use anyway. He names Beehiiv, GoHighLevel, ClickFunnels, Stripe, and says he made "really really good money" this way — mostly off YouTube tutorials with links in the description, because long-form how-to has natural link placement. KDP-adjacent equivalents: cover mockup tools, keyword/rank trackers, Amazon ads tools, print services.
- **Sponsorship packaging:** refused one-off ~$1,000 reel deals; pushed everyone to retainers — *"4–5 reels a month for $4,000/month."* YouTube rate $4–5K/video. One deal was pure production: one video a day, delivered to the client, posted on *their* account, never on his. You can sell content production without selling your feed.
- **Why brands chased him — and the warning attached.** He was in tech/SaaS, where sponsors have more money than they know what to do with and are desperate for distribution, and his content was how-to/informational so product integration felt natural. **He explicitly says this does not transfer to niches where the companies don't have money.** All inbound; he never did outbound; they scraped his profile and emailed one of ~4 addresses, so he monitors every inbox and his DM requests.
- **"A reel is basically a thread I'm now saying out loud."** He learned copywriting as a reply guy on pre-algorithm Twitter, where a thread's first tweet had 240 characters to earn the read with no thumbnail, no title, no edit tricks. Anyone with text-platform reps already has the reel skill.
- **Negative signal on clipping.** He says clipper armies do *not* work for talking-head software content — nothing flashy, nothing clippable — and that the arbitrage window is closing.

## 6. Yoni — 176K IG, 420 days straight, app made $26K in month one

- **The follower-count curiosity loop — the one growth hack explicitly designed for a tiny account.** State your live follower count in the video: *"Today we have 287 followers, that's up from 271 yesterday. I'm posting every day, see you tomorrow."* Viewers go to your profile to check the *current* number, which is now higher — profile visit converts to follow. He says it may have driven his early growth. Florian's caveat, which matters for Antoine: if nothing is growing, this backfires — pick the metric that *is* moving (books generated, days shipped, waitlist, MRR once it exists).
- **First video ever, 5 seconds of effort, borrowing a trend to mock it:** *"Day 1 of doing nothing for every follower I gain. Follow me to find out."* Filmed in the Instagram camera, no edits, hidden somewhere nobody could see him.
- **Post the ugly ratio — the accidental-controversy engine.** His first ~2M-view video: a time-lapse of him working at 3am plus the real numbers on screen — *"so far I've made $8.80 and I've spent $4,000."* The comment section piled on ("look at this idiot," "you're better off selling dirt") and the pile-on is what carried the reach. His read: you need some controversy, and the honest early-stage loss *is* the controversy. Antoine has a stack of these: €0 MRR, 2 lifetime customers, hours built, the ban.
- **The rejection series.** One-to-two-million-view videos that were literally *"my app got rejected for the fifth time."* Six App Store rejections over ~2 months, each one a post. His argument for build-in-public is specifically about the *pre-launch struggle*: either you build in silence and nobody cares, or the rejections themselves build the tension. Antoine's equivalent inventory: KDP rejections, ban appeals, content-policy fights, metadata/compliance rules that make no sense.
- **CapCut transcript editing — the single biggest time-saver named in all six episodes.** Pro feature: CapCut transcribes the video, you delete text from the transcript, it cuts the corresponding video. No scrubbing to find the flubbed takes. **Took him from ~2 hours per video down to ~30 minutes.** (Descript pioneered it; CapCut copied it.) His full loop: pick a topic, no script, talk into a DJI Osmo Pocket for 3–5 minutes, cut to 60 seconds, post.
- **Streak insurance:** before a 2–3 week trip to Japan/Hawaii he spent 3–4 days filming **30 videos** and scheduled them out. Directly what Postiz is for.
- **Operational warning for a Postiz user: TikTok penalises API publishing.** TikTok wants in-app posting and deprioritises scheduler-posted content. Florian confirms he suspects filtering on his own account. Also: your IP geo-targets your distribution (his Bali IP kept sending his English content to Indonesian viewers).
- **Product Hunt, with the actual lever named.** He scheduled the launch the night before, woke up at #4 by 7am, asked his followers, went 4→3→2→1, got 10,000 downloads in a day. The real driver wasn't the ranking — it was **Product Hunt's daily newsletter**, which gave him the entire top slot with a banner and a subject line about his product ("cure your brain rot"). **Actionable:** the PH newsletter carries a line saying *"know a project deserving the spotlight? email us"* (editors@producthunt.com). Contact the editors *ahead* of your launch and ask what it takes to get spotlighted. Revenue split: ~$3K from his own audience, ~$5K from Product Hunt, then a steady ~$200/day.
- **He named the product after a search term people already type.** 300–500 downloads/day of sustained organic from people searching "brain rot" in the App Store. DraftToDone doesn't own a query — but the *content* can.
- **Ask the audience to make a concrete visual decision.** "Should the hot tub go here, or the hammocks here?" People answer in volume because they want to be part of it. Florian confirms: he posted a photo of his office asking what would make it look better and got flooded. Antoine's version doubles as product research: *"which of these 3 covers should I ship?" / "which niche should the next book target?"*
- **Pinned public goals list + periodic status updates, including the misses.** Pinned to profile: $1M revenue / $1K MRR software / 3 properties / 100K followers / 12 books / 265lb bench. Mid-year update posted publicly: tracking $650K not $1M, 4 books not 12, 240lb not 265. The mix of business and personal goals is what makes it human, and the honest misses are the content.
- **Talk about the money the moment there is any.** His "$26K in 30 days" thread did 1.2M impressions on X, a platform where he was otherwise small. The flywheel: revenue → Starter Story, Greg Isenberg, podcasts come to *you*. Caveat: at €0 MRR Antoine doesn't have this yet, which is exactly why the ban + the loss numbers are the substitute.
- **Negative signal on UGC:** he tried UGC creators, TikTok, main pages — none worked. He names the market rate ($10–30/video, college-age creators, ~100 videos/month) and notes that Cal AI ($3M/mo) skips UGC for real influencer marketing. Not a channel for a solo founder with no budget.

## Top 3 things Antoine should actually do

**1. Run "10 KDP book packages in 10 hours," live or timelapsed, and publish the raw recording.**
Florin Pop's 10-apps-in-10-hours took his channel 30K→60K in one month off a single 2.3M-view video, and he decided on it that same morning with zero prep. This is the only format in the batch where DraftToDone's actual output *is* the spectacle — the product ships a finished package on a timer, so the challenge and the demo are the same artifact. It also satisfies Gosha's test for standing out in a saturated build-in-public feed: an absurd, time-boxed, publicly-committed target. Post each finished package to X/Threads as it completes (Florin posted each app as he finished), then cut the recording into a long-form video plus 10 shorts. One day of work produces a month of content and a live proof-of-product.

**2. Ship a monthly "3–5 KDP niches worth publishing into" email and post, with one CTA at the bottom.**
Alex Aiden's identical format ("here's 3–5 software ideas for you to run with") booked calls *every single time* he sent it, and closed easily. Each niche gets: why it was picked, how you'd take it to market, how hard it is to execute — then one line: *"want DraftToDone to produce the whole package? here."* This is the known "edgy selling" tactic in its most concrete form, it's the exact job-to-be-done of the KDP catalog operator, it gives the newsletter a reason to exist, and it is the highest-signal thing a metadata-obsessed prosumer can receive from you. It also produces the bio-link promise Alex used to hit 1,000 subscribers/day: make the bio read *"free monthly: 3–5 KDP niches worth publishing into,"* not "AI book SaaS."

**3. Lock a standing anchor hook + a destination sign-off, and lead with the ugly numbers, not the milestones.**
Keegan restates the same anchor sentence with the same number on every video; Yoni's biggest early video was "I've made $8.80 and spent $4,000," and his million-view videos were "my app got rejected for the fifth time." Antoine's anchor writes itself: *"I built a KDP catalog to €400/month, then Amazon banned me permanently over a joke pen name. Day N of rebuilding it as software."* Sign-off is a destination, never "follow me." Then post the losses on purpose — €0 MRR, 2 lifetime customers, the ban appeal, the compliance rules that make no sense — because that's the controversy engine and he has an inventory of it. This replaces "share milestones," which he has none of yet.

*Runner-up, and the only tactic with a guaranteed floor at zero audience:* Rajan's routine of opening the profile of every new commenter and DMing them something specific about it. He got to 100–150 automatic commenters on every post this way, and his advice to a beginner is literally "your first goal should be to connect one-on-one with the first 20 new people." Costs nothing, works at 12 followers.

## What NOT to copy from these episodes

- **Do not translate "watch the cabin get built" into "watch the SaaS get built."** Rajan and Keegan's entire premise is a physically photogenic, expensive, progressive build — a bulldozer, a drone, a room that visibly changes. The medium *is* the content. Software has no bulldozer, and Gosha says the SaaS-flavoured version of this (POV / day-in-the-life / build-in-public packaging) is precisely the saturated red ocean where new creators now look interchangeable.
- **Do not adopt the "keep the brand pure / wait for them to ask" posture.** Rajan refuses all money and lives with his parents; Gosha wants you to wait until the community requests a product; Keegan says monetizing now would be "a disservice." All three can afford to wait — one has a real-estate deal, one has an agency, one has a full-time job. Antoine is at €0 MRR with 2 customers. The correct read of Gosha's evidence is *"put the product on screen instead of pitching it,"* not *"don't sell for a year."*
- **Do not plan on brand deals.** Alex Aiden made 5 figures/month from sponsorships and then explicitly disclaimed it: he was in tech/SaaS where sponsors have unlimited budgets and no distribution. KDP-tool companies are small and cash-poor, and Antoine's audience will be tiny for a long time. Florin Pop's inbound-vs-outbound rule seals it — chasing sponsors means low rates and creative interference.
- **Do not buy clippers or UGC.** Alex says clipping doesn't work for talking-head software content and the arbitrage is closing. Yoni actually tried UGC creators, TikTok pages, and main pages and got nothing. Both are cash-out, results-unproven for this exact content type.
- **Do not copy Gosha's lifestyle-keyword aesthetic.** "Zero mention of the product vocabulary, sell Bali and digital-nomad imagery" works on consumer-aspiration audiences. The KDP catalog operator buys repeatability, metadata control, and not-getting-banned. Lifestyle-first packaging reads as guru-bait to exactly the person Antoine needs and repels the compliance-minded operator who is his ICP.
- **Do not assume Instagram is the platform because it was for them.** 325K IG vs 92K TikTok vs 9K YouTube is a fact about *real-estate reels*, not about prosumer software. The KDP operator lives on YouTube long-form, Reddit, Facebook groups, and email. Instagram is the wrong default here.
- **Do not budget for the aesthetic tier.** Gosha's floor is $600–700/mo (editor + music + camera) and Rajan spends 6–7 hours a day on a single 60-second video. Alex Aiden's ~5 hours *per month* for 1–2 posts/day is the only number in this batch compatible with also building and selling a SaaS. Copy Alex's economics, not Rajan's craft.
- **Do not treat "audience first, product first" as settled by Yoni's answer.** He says audience-first, but he had a full-time software engineering salary funding 420 days of posting and 12–18 prior failed businesses. Antoine has a finished product and needs revenue now; his sequencing constraint is not Yoni's.
- **Skip the "get a cast" advice.** Structurally unavailable to a solo founder. The only affordable version is putting customers or other KDP operators on camera — worth doing, but it's a different tactic than what Gosha describes.
