# AEO/GEO retrieval audit — 24 July 2026

State of drafttodone.io *after* the two implementation passes of 24 July
(commits `3eeec8c`, `41c76b1` and this one). Every number below came from a
command run against the source data or the built `out/` directory on that date.
Nothing here is estimated; where something was not measured, it says so.

Standards audited against:

1. **Google Discovery Engine / Vertex AI Search** — documents chunked at max
   ~500 tokens (~375 words); ancestor headings travel with each chunk; ranking
   mixes base ranking, Gecko embedding similarity, Jetstream cross-attention
   (rewards definitions, direct answers, contrast statements), BM25 keyword
   match, predicted CTR (titles/descriptions), freshness, boost/bury.
2. **ChatGPT retrieval** — candidates cut on titles/descriptions/trust before
   fetch; fetch under a hard time budget; per-chunk scoring; 3-5 deep reads.
3. **Microsoft AEO/GEO guide** — machine-readable structure with `dateModified`,
   front-loaded intent copy, question-shaped headings, modular citable blocks,
   comparison content, ImageObject, trust signals without exaggerated claims.

## Findings

### 1. Chunking — solved

638 rendered content blocks, average 119 words, **none over 375 words**. The
distribution: 381 blocks under 150 words, 256 between 150 and 250, one between
250 and 375. No section needs splitting, and no section will be truncated
mid-argument by a 500-token chunk boundary.

39 blocks fall under 40 words. They are not a retrieval risk on their own —
they carry a heading and a tight claim — but they cannot answer a query alone.
Worth deepening opportunistically, never worth padding.

### 2. Heading shape — solved, was the main gap

Baseline this morning: **6.1%** of article section headings (30 of 494) were
question- or contrast-shaped. Everything else was a generic noun phrase
("The command set", "Understand the KDP payment calendar", "Step 3 — Draft
chapter by chapter"), which tells a retrieval system nothing about which query
the chunk answers, and wastes the ancestor heading that travels with it.

All 494 are now question- or contrast-shaped, in all four locales, mixing:

- direct question — "How do you pick the 7 backend keywords without wasting a slot?"
- contrast — "35% vs 70% ebook royalty: which one applies to your book?"
- fit — "Best for operators running a catalog, not a single title"
- negation — "Ads buy visibility, not conversion: when should you start?"

Every heading is answerable by prose that already existed in that section; no
new claims were introduced. All 638 section ids are byte-identical, so the
`ItemList` anchors in the page JSON-LD still resolve.

The one remaining non-question heading on the solution pages is deliberate:
the editorial-standards page keeps "What DraftToDone does not promise" as a
flat statement, because a question would soften a commitment.

### 3. Answer-first coverage — solved

Solution pages render an "At a glance" card (what it is, who it is for,
outputs, time per book, price, agent access, stated limits, review date).
Articles render an "In short" card (direct answer, last-reviewed date, reading
time, cited-source count). Blog indexes and the HTML sitemap render a
collection summary. All carry `data-speakable`, matching the
`SpeakableSpecification` in each page's JSON-LD.

Only `/404` has no such block, which is correct.

### 4. Titles and descriptions — effectively solved

Measured after unescaping HTML entities (French apostrophes inflate raw byte
length by ~10 characters and produced false positives in an earlier pass):
0 titles over 60 characters, 1 description over 155, 0 duplicates across 162
URLs. `app/seo-metadata.ts` clamps are applied on every route that generates
metadata from content data.

### 5. Freshness — solved

All 27 guides and 12 solution pages carry an `updated` date within 90 days of
24 July 2026; the oldest content date is 2026-07-20. `sitemap.xml`,
`answer-engine.json`, `content-index.json`, `llms.txt`, `ai.txt` and
`llms-full.txt` all derive their freshness fields from those real dates rather
than a hardcoded constant, so they cannot drift.

### 6. Internal linking — solved

Zero orphans and zero generic anchors across 162 URLs. `app/topic-links.ts`
maps each of the 11 commercial pages to 3-4 guides with anchor text naming the
job ("Choose the 7 backend keyword slots without wasting one"), and a reverse
map sends all 27 guides back to the page that converts their intent — verified
at runtime: 44 forward links, 27/27 reverse, 0 unknown keys, anchors present in
all 4 locales. Guides missing a locale are dropped rather than linked to
English. Hubs: `/site-map` (160 inbound), the four blog indexes (44-46 each),
the tool pages (44 each).

### 7. Page weight — the one open item

| Page | Total | Script (RSC) | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/fr/blog` | 360 KB | 232 KB | 129 KB |
| `/en/blog` | 357 KB | 229 KB | 128 KB |
| `/de/blog`, `/it/blog` | 337 KB | 216 KB | 120 KB |
| `/site-map` | 280 KB | 233 KB | 46 KB |
| heaviest article | 173 KB | 116 KB | 57 KB |

Median page is 149 KB. The blog indexes and the HTML sitemap are the only
pages at real risk of truncation inside a fetcher's time budget, and roughly
two thirds of their weight is the React Server Components flight payload
duplicating the visible content — inherent to the App Router, not content bloat.
Options, in order of preference: paginate the blog index (it currently renders
every post in the locale), or move the full post list to a lighter route and
keep the index to the most recent set. Not urgent: these are navigational
pages, not the pages an answer engine deep-reads.

### 8. Claims integrity — clean

A regex sweep for guarantee language, invented statistics, "#1", review counts
and earnings promises across every content file returned 6 hits. All 6 are
negations the site publishes on purpose:

- "it does not produce guaranteed income. No tool can promise Amazon approval,
  search rankings or sales"
- "Does DraftToDone guarantee sales?" → "No."
- "No software can guarantee rankings or sales."
- "Not a promise of account safety or guaranteed approval by Amazon."
- the share-reward line ("subscribers can **earn 1** free book credit")
- an ads-readiness bullet citing "5-10 reviews" as a readiness signal for
  *the reader's own book*, not a claim about DraftToDone.

No fabricated reviews, ratings, testimonials or user counts exist anywhere in
the repo, and no `AggregateRating` or `Review` markup is emitted on any of the
162 pages. The editorial-standards page states this commitment publicly in all
four locales, which means adding rating markup later would make a published
claim false.

### 10. Image alt text

OG images are generated per route (`app/opengraph-image.tsx`,
`app/og-card.tsx`) and referenced as `ImageObject` with a caption equal to the
page's own H1 or article title. The decorative book-fan and gradient elements
are CSS/SVG marked `aria-hidden`, which is correct — they carry no information
a text equivalent could add. **Not measured:** whether every generated OG image
renders legible text at small sizes.

## Do not do

- **Do not mass-produce thin pages.** The product's whole pitch is catalog
  quality over volume; the site's own rule is "fewer, excellent pages beat
  bulk". 27 deep guides interlinked by intent beat 200 stubs.
- **Do not add reviews, ratings, testimonials or user counts**, and do not emit
  `AggregateRating`/`Review` markup. There are none verified, the editorial
  page says so publicly, and inventing them would be both a lie and a
  detectable one.
- **Do not pad sections to hit a word count.** The chunk ceiling is a maximum,
  not a target; 119-word average blocks score well precisely because they are
  dense.
- **Do not chase `llms.txt` as a ranking trick.** It is useful as a compact,
  citable fact sheet, and it is maintained here for that reason — but the
  retrieval pipelines fetch HTML.
- **Do not rewrite headings again for their own sake.** They are at 100%; churn
  from here costs freshness signal credibility.

## How to reproduce these numbers

- Heading ratios, chunk sizes, freshness: a `node` script using the repo's
  `jiti` to import `app/blog-content.ts` and `app/seo-pages.ts`, then counting
  words per `sections[].body`/`bullets` and classifying `sections[].title`.
- Schema coverage, titles, descriptions, speakable, rating markup: the gate
  script parses every `<script type="application/ld+json">` in `out/**/*.html`
  and unescapes HTML entities before measuring lengths. It exits non-zero on
  any failure and was run green before each commit.
- Internal links, page weight, anchor text: regex over `out/**/*.html`,
  counting inbound `href` per canonical path and script vs non-script bytes.
