# Niche brief templates

A brief is not a topic. A topic produces a generic book that nobody finishes. A brief names a
reader, a moment, and a promise — and that is what makes a book worth selling.

From the [KDP Operator's Playbook](https://drafttodone.io/en/kdp-operator-playbook).

---

## The shape of a brief that works

Five fields. Fill all five before you generate anything.

| Field | What it is | Why it matters |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| **Reader** | Who specifically, in one line | A book for "everyone" reads like it was written for nobody |
| **Moment** | What just happened that made them search | This is what they type into Amazon |
| **Promise** | What is true for them after reading | This becomes the title and the first line of the description |
| **Proof of demand** | The evidence you found that people buy here | Stops you writing into an empty market |
| **Shape** | Chapter count, length, format | Sets expectations the book actually meets |

---

## Template — copy this

```
READER
  Who: ...............................................................
  What they already know: ............................................
  What they have already tried: ......................................

MOMENT
  The thing that just happened: ......................................
  What they typed into Amazon: .......................................

PROMISE
  After this book they can: ..........................................
  In how long: .......................................................
  What this book will NOT do: ........................................

PROOF OF DEMAND
  Top 3 competing titles: ............................................
  Most recent review date on those: ..................................
  What the 3-star reviews complain about: ............................
  The gap my book fills: .............................................

SHAPE
  Chapters: .....  Target length: .....  Format: ebook / paperback / both
  Tone: ..............................................................
```

## A worked example — the difference is the whole thing

**Weak brief (a topic):**

> A book about sourdough baking.

Generates a competent, generic sourdough book. Competes against a thousand identical ones.
Ranks for nothing. Sells nothing.

**Strong brief (a reader, a moment, a promise):**

```
READER
  Who: Someone who has baked sourdough 5-10 times and keeps getting flat, dense loaves.
  What they already know: How to feed a starter. Basic terminology.
  What they have already tried: Watching videos, changing flour, longer proofing.

MOMENT
  The thing that just happened: Their fourth flat loaf in a row. They are about to quit.
  What they typed into Amazon: "why is my sourdough flat"

PROMISE
  After this book they can: Diagnose which of the 6 causes made THIS loaf flat, and fix it.
  In how long: Their next bake.
  What this book will NOT do: Teach them to start from zero. This assumes they have a starter.

PROOF OF DEMAND
  Top 3 competing titles: [three real titles, checked on Amazon]
  Most recent review date on those: within the last 6 weeks -> the market is actively buying
  What the 3-star reviews complain about: "too basic", "assumes you're a beginner",
    "doesn't explain WHY it failed"
  The gap my book fills: Diagnosis, not instruction. Troubleshooting for people past step one.

SHAPE
  Chapters: 8   Target length: 25,000   Format: both
  Tone: Direct, diagnostic. Like a mechanic explaining what went wrong.
```

That brief writes its own title, its own subtitle, its own keywords, and its own description —
because the reader, the moment and the promise are already stated in the reader's own words.

## Where the fields come from

**Reader and moment** — from the review sections of competing books. Buyers describe their
situation in reviews far more honestly than they do in any survey.

**Promise** — from the 3-star reviews of the top sellers. Three-star reviewers liked the book
enough to finish it and still felt something was missing. They are naming your book for you.

**Proof of demand** — recent reviews mean recent buyers. A category where the top titles have
no reviews in six months is a category that has stopped buying, however good the search volume
looks.

**What this book will NOT do** — the most skipped field, and the one that most improves the
result. It prevents scope drift, it prevents the generated manuscript from restating basics,
and it becomes a line in your description that reduces refunds.

## Ten brief starts, to break the blank page

Each of these is a *moment*, not a topic. That is the point.

1. They have just been told to cut sugar and have no idea what to actually eat on Monday.
2. They have inherited a house with a garden they do not know how to maintain.
3. Their dog started resource-guarding last week and they are frightened of it.
4. They have been made a manager on Friday and start on Monday with no training.
5. Their first rental guest left a 3-star review and they do not know why.
6. They have been told to prepare for a colonoscopy and the leaflet was not enough.
7. Their teenager has stopped talking to them and they do not know when it started.
8. They have six weeks until a wedding and want to look like themselves, only rested.
9. They just got a diagnosis and everything online is either terrifying or useless.
10. They are moving to a country where they do not speak the language, in five weeks.
