# The metadata worksheet

The manuscript decides whether readers keep the book. The metadata decides whether they ever
see it. This is the sheet that gets filled in before you touch the KDP upload form.

From the [KDP Operator's Playbook](https://drafttodone.io/en/kdp-operator-playbook).

## How to fill each field

### Title — the promise, not the topic

The title's job is to make a specific person feel recognised. "Sourdough Baking" is a topic.
"Why Your Sourdough Is Flat" is a promise, and it is what the reader typed.

Test: read the title alone. Can you tell who it is for and what changes for them? If not,
it is a topic.

### Subtitle — the keywords, in a sentence

The subtitle is where the search phrases live, and it must still read as English. Amazon
indexes it; humans read it. Both have to work.

A subtitle that reads as a keyword list signals low quality to buyers and does not rank better
than one that reads naturally.

### Description — the first two lines are the whole thing

Most buyers read two lines and decide. Those two lines must contain the outcome.

Not: *"Written by an experienced baker with twenty years of…"* — nobody cares yet.
Instead: *"Your loaf is flat because of one of six things. This book tells you which one, and
what to change before your next bake."*

Then bullets. Then, near the end, one honest line about who should not buy it. That line
reduces refunds and reads as confidence.

### The 7 slots — phrases, and seven of them

Two mistakes account for most wasted slots:

1. **Using words instead of phrases.** "sourdough" is a word. "why is my sourdough flat" is a
   phrase someone typed.
2. **Repeating.** If the word appears in the title, it is already indexed. Repeating it in a
   slot buys nothing and costs you a slot. Seven distinct phrases is seven distinct entry points.

Where to find them: Amazon's own search bar. Start typing the job your book solves and read the
autocomplete. That list is real search behaviour.

### Categories — pick what you can win

Ranking in a category you can win beats invisibility in one you cannot. Open the category, look
at the current #10 book, and ask honestly whether your book can outperform it. If not, go
narrower.

## The metadata review, 5 minutes before upload

- [ ] Title and subtitle do not repeat each other
- [ ] The description's first two lines state the outcome
- [ ] All 7 slots used, all distinct, all phrases
- [ ] Categories are winnable
- [ ] Pen name searched and clear
- [ ] Price checked against the royalty band and printing cost
